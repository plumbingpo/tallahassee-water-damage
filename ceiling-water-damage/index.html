Ceiling Water Damage Repair in Tallahassee

A brown ring on the ceiling is the last thing to show up, not the first. Before any mark shows, water has traveled down a joist, saturated the insulation and collected on top of the drywall. The leak may be a bathroom on the floor above, a pipe in the ceiling, an air conditioner drain line or the roof. Nothing is repaired until the origin of the water has been tracked down.

Painting over the stain does not fix it. Moisture left in the cavity brings the discoloration back and lets mold get going on the paper side in 24 to 48 hours. We open only what we need to, pull out wet insulation, dry the joists and remaining drywall, then patch, texture and paint so the repair blends in. If the ceiling has started to droop, treat it differently. Stay out from under it and call.

Warning Signs

  • Stains or streaks running down the wall just below the ceiling line
  • Stale, mildew like odor you cannot trace to anything you can see
  • A ceiling panel that droops, bulges or gives under light pressure
  • Water dripping from a light fixture, vent or smoke detector
  • Thin cracking in the ceiling finish centered on a wet looking area
  • Ceiling texture crumbling off or changing color in one section

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

What the Upstairs Is Hiding

A wet ceiling usually means a wet subfloor or attic deck directly over it. We check that level as well, because drying only the ceiling leaves moisture behind.

Mold Above You

Mold usually grows out of sight on the upper face of the panel and in the insulation above, both of which hold moisture well after the painted side dries.

Asbestos in Older Ceilings

Popcorn texture and plaster from past decades may have asbestos in them. Testing comes first in such a home, ahead of any cutting, scraping or demolition of the damaged section.

Electricity Overhead

Recessed lights, fans and junction boxes sit right in the wet zone. A short is possible when water enters a light, which is why the breaker should be shut off.

How We Fix It

Our Ceiling Water Damage Repair Process

1

Trace the Leak

We track the water back to where it starts using moisture meters and thermal imaging, checking the room or attic above. The leak has to be stopped before repairs make sense.

2

Secure the Room

We cut power to any wet fixture and move or cover the furniture underneath. If the ceiling is holding water, we release it in a controlled way and catch it.

3

Open and Dry

Wet insulation is removed along with any drywall that cannot stay. Air movers and a dehumidifier dry the joists and surrounding ceiling, and we apply antimicrobial where the water was not clean.

4

Rebuild and Paint

Once moisture readings are normal we install new insulation and drywall, tape and finish the seams, match the existing texture and prime with a stain blocker before painting.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

First hours

Water Spreads Above

The insulation gets wet as water travels down the framing. From inside the room everything can appear normal, since the drywall is taking on water on the hidden face.

24 to 48 hours

Marks Start Showing

You see the first ring, along with blisters in the paint. Soaked insulation weighs on the drywall while mold can start on its paper face.

Day 3 to 7

The Ceiling Bows

Drywall softens and pulls away from its screws. The ceiling bows between joists, seams open up and a musty odor reaches the room.

Past one week

Sections May Fall

Parts of the ceiling can come down along with the wet insulation resting on them. The repair zone is far bigger at this stage, with mold well rooted inside the cavity.
