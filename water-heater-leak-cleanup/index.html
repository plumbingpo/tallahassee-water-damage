First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Cut the Power or GasElectric units get shut down at the breaker. For gas, turn the control knob to off. Never leave a heating element or burner running on an empty tank.
  2. Close the Cold SupplyFind the cold water shutoff above the heater and turn it clockwise. A stuck or leaking valve means going to the house main and shutting the water off there.
  3. Beware of Scalding WaterTank water is hot enough to scald. Let pooled water cool off before anyone steps in it, and keep kids and pets out of the area.
  4. Drain the Tank SafelyWhen the drain valve is within reach, connect a garden hose and lead it to a floor drain or outdoors, which keeps the rest of the water off the floor.
  5. Move Stored ItemsLift boxes, rugs and anything absorbent off the wet floor. Fabric and cardboard absorb water fast, then keep it pressed to the concrete and walls.
The Usual Culprits

What Causes It

  • The drain valve at the bottom of the tank cracking, loosening or failing to shut fully.
  • Drain pans beneath closet or attic units that rust through or clog and then spill over.
  • Loose plumbing connections left after a recent installation or repair.
  • A temperature and pressure relief valve that opens or fails and discharges water.
  • Pressure rising too high in the tank because the thermostat failed or no expansion tank was installed.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Stand and Subfloor Damage

More water goes into the subfloor or wooden stand beneath the tank than anywhere else. It can rot and weaken, which matters when a new full tank is set on it.

Attic and Ceilings

Water from an attic unit runs down through insulation to the ceiling beneath it. Wet insulation stops insulating and its weight can bring ceiling drywall down.

Closet Walls

When the heater closet is indoors, its drywall draws water upward and passes it to rooms on the far side. Swollen baseboards and a wall bottom that is still damp within are typical.

Garage Wall Base

The wall between the garage and the house takes water into its bottom plate and drywall from the slab, and from there it reaches the floors of the living space.

What We Do

Our Water Heater Leak Cleanup Process

1

Extract the Water

Our crew extracts the water standing in the closet, the garage or on the floor, plus the overflow pan and the cavity above the ceiling on attic jobs, so that materials around it quit absorbing it.

2

Check Around the Heater

To learn the full reach of the water, our crew meters and scans the platform, the bottoms of the walls, neighboring rooms and the ceiling below if there is one.

3

Take Out What Will Not Dry

We take out baseboards that are damaged, drywall that is crumbling and insulation that is soaked. In attics we clear wet insulation so the ceiling joists and drywall below can be dried.

4

Drying and Moisture Readings

Drying usually takes 3 to 5 days, and dehumidifiers and air movers run the whole time. Our crew logs the readings and lets you know as soon as the area is ready for a replacement heater.

Coverage

Insurance and Water Heater Leak Cleanup

A sudden tank failure is often treated as accidental and sudden, so the resulting damage to belongings, floors and walls is often covered. The water heater itself usually is not, since wearing out with age is considered maintenance. Because coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks, a claim may be denied or reduced when the adjuster sees indications the tank leaked for a long time, like old stains or trails of rust. Check your policy, photograph the heater and the damage, and keep the old unit until the claim is settled.

Pricing

What Drives the Price

  • Placement of the unit, as leaks from an attic or an inside closet run higher than those in a garage.
  • Whether flooring in adjoining rooms was reached and what type it is.
  • Any rebuilding of the subfloor or platform beneath the tank ahead of the new heater.
  • The amount of wall drywall, ceiling drywall or insulation that has to be removed.
