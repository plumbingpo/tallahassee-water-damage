Kitchen Water Damage: What Tallahassee Homeowners Should Know

No room has as many water connections as the kitchen, with its faucet, drain, disposal, dishwasher and refrigerator line. Almost every one sits out of view, behind an appliance or inside a cabinet. A drip from any of them travels to the rear of the cabinet base, then spreads beneath the flooring. We help Tallahassee homeowners find it, dry it, and save as much of the kitchen as possible.

In a wet kitchen, the cabinets are where the real expense lies. Particleboard boxes swell fast and stay swollen, but solid wood and plywood frequently dry well. Toe kicks come off and a few small, concealed holes are drilled so dry air can travel behind and below the cabinets, which lets cabinets and countertops stay put in many kitchens during drying.

Warning Signs

  • Doors on the cabinets that have gone out of alignment or started sticking
  • Swollen or darkened toe kicks, or ones separating from the base of the cabinets
  • A sink cabinet bottom that feels soft, looks stained, or has warped
  • Downstairs, a brownish mark on the ceiling beneath the kitchen
  • Swelling and flaking where the cabinet sides meet the floor
  • Laminate or wood floor that is lifting or cupping where you stand at the dishwasher
Our Process

How We Handle Kitchen Water Damage

1

Shut Off the Leak

We shut off the leaking fixture or appliance line, pull the dishwasher or refrigerator if needed, and confirm exactly which connection failed so it can be repaired before drying begins.

2

Map the Moisture

The base of each cabinet, the wall in back and flooring all the way across the kitchen are checked with meters and a thermal camera. Under kitchen flooring, water often goes farther than anyone expects.

3

Dry Without Demolition

With the toe kicks off, air gets pushed below the cabinets and behind them. An antimicrobial is applied to damp cavities while air movers and dehumidifiers work over several days.

4

Remove Only Damage

We carefully remove any flooring, subfloor or cabinet section that cannot be saved. After dry readings are confirmed, the appliances go back in and we help you arrange the remaining repairs.

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

The Wall Behind the Cabinets

The wall at the back of the sink and dishwasher holds drain and supply pipes. When they leak, framing and drywall get wet behind the cabinets, and mold spreads where no one can see it.

Behind the Dishwasher

A slow drip at the supply or drain connection wets the subfloor and the cabinet sides next to it. Until the floor changes, the dishwasher covers everything.

Sink Drain Leaks

Because it leaks just when the sink drains, a loose fitting or a worn disposal seal often goes unnoticed. The water carries food waste, which is why a wet sink cabinet smells and molds so fast.

Flooring Over Flooring

It is common for a kitchen floor to be laid right over the previous one. Between those layers water is trapped, unable to evaporate and invisible from above.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

The first hour

Water Goes Underneath

When a hose or fitting fails, water heads behind the cabinets and beneath the appliances. Through edges and seams, it works its way under the floor covering.

24 to 48 hours

Cabinets Start Swelling

Particleboard cabinet bases and toe kicks soak up water and swell. Mold can begin in the dark, closed space under the cabinets and behind the dishwasher.

Days 3 through 7

Floors Begin Moving

As adhesive releases, vinyl begins lifting, while hardwood boards cup and laminate edges swell. The doors and drawers on the cabinets go out of alignment.

After a week

Tear Out Likely

Swollen cabinet boxes cannot be brought back. The subfloor may be coming apart in layers, and mold behind cabinets often means they come out together with the countertop.
