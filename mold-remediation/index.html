How to Prevent Mold Remediation Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Mold in ductwork is prevented by servicing the heating and cooling system and replacing its filters on schedule
  • Use mold-resistant drywall and paint in moisture-prone areas like bathrooms and basements
  • Overflowing gutters send water into basements and crawl spaces, so clean them on a schedule
  • Make sure bathrooms, kitchens and laundry areas have adequate exhaust ventilation
  • Mold can get going after just 24 to 48 hours of dampness, so have leaks fixed within 24 hours
  • When windows, piping or other cold surfaces start to sweat, correct it without waiting

The Benefits of Professional Mold Remediation

Mold remediation is not a DIY project. When a colony is disturbed and nothing contains it, millions of spores go into the air, where they may settle in clean rooms and expose your family more than the mold did before. Professional mold remediation uses negative air pressure containment, HEPA-filtered air scrubbers and personal protective equipment so spores are captured and contained during removal.

Professional mold remediation also addresses the root cause of the growth, which is the moisture source. Clean off the visible growth but leave the moisture problem, and the mold comes back, in many cases in a matter of weeks. Our certified technicians are trained and equipped to track down moisture you cannot see, dry the affected areas the right way and make the corrections that keep mold from recurring.

How to Tell You Need Mold Remediation

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Water damage not long ago that was left to dry without professional equipment
  • Headaches, allergy flare-ups or breathing symptoms that ease whenever you leave the house
  • Peeling wallpaper, bubbling paint or warped materials that point to hidden moisture
  • Persistent musty or earthy odors, especially in enclosed or humid areas
  • Furniture against outside walls or items in closets developing speckled patches
  • Windows, cold surfaces or walls that sweat with condensation on a regular basis
  • Long-running hidden leaks, for example at a dishwasher line, sink drain or roof flashing
