How to Waterproof Your Basement in Tallahassee, FL

Home / Blog / How to Waterproof Your Basement in Tallahassee, FL

A dry basement protects your home's structure, the air you breathe indoors, and what the property is worth. Many homes in Tallahassee, FL still deal with basement moisture, anywhere from light seepage to serious flooding. This guide from Tallahassee Restoration Co covers the usual causes of basement water intrusion and the fixes that keep Tallahassee basements dry and healthy.

How Water Gets Into a Basement

Water gets into basements in several ways, and knowing the source is key to picking the right fix. Hydrostatic pressure builds when saturated soil around your foundation pushes water through cracks, joints, and even porous concrete itself. Lateral pressure from expanding clay soil can crack foundation walls and let water in. If the yard slopes the wrong way, gutters are blocked, or drainage is inadequate, rain collects at the foundation and works its way inside. A basement can also be damp without any leak at all, because humid warm air condenses on cool walls and floors.

Outside Fixes for a Wet Basement

Long term, nothing works as well as handling the water outside, before it reaches the wall. Exterior foundation waterproofing means excavating around the foundation, applying waterproof membrane coatings, and installing or upgrading the exterior drain tile. This option costs more than any other, yet it fixes the cause and protects the basement more completely than the alternatives. Regrading is also worth the money, because ground that falls 1 inch or more per foot across the first 6 feet away from the foundation carries water off.

Interior Waterproofing Solutions

Interior waterproofing systems manage water that gets through the foundation instead of keeping it from reaching the foundation. Water that reaches the basement perimeter is caught by an interior French drain, which carries it to the sump pump to be discharged. A sump pump, preferably one backed up by a battery, handles the water reliably when storms hit or the power goes out. Sealants and waterproof coatings applied inside help with light seepage and efflorescence, yet they are no substitute for real drainage if a lot of water is coming in.

Keeping Your Sump Pump Ready

For Tallahassee homeowners with sump pumps, proper maintenance is essential. Four times a year, pour a bucket of water in the sump pit and watch for the pump to start. Listen for unusual sounds that could point to mechanical problems. Install a battery backup to stay protected during power outages, the most common time for basement flooding. For more protection still, think about a second, water-driven pump. The discharge line should end 6 feet or more away from the foundation, with a check valve fitted so water cannot flow back.
