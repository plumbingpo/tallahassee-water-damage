Dealing With Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in Tallahassee

A sump pump has one job, and it tends to fail at the moment it is needed most. During heavy rain the water table climbs and fills the pit, at which point an outage, a stuck float or an overwhelmed pump stops the pumping. The pit overflows and the basement floor starts to flood. It keeps coming until a pump is running again.

Clean is the wrong word for what comes out of a sump pit. It has come through soil and often carries silt, bacteria and whatever was in the drain tile, so we treat it as Category 2 at best and Category 3 if it has sat or mixed with sewage. Cleanup is different as a result, with wet drywall, carpet pad and other porous materials generally removed and the rest cleaned and disinfected ahead of drying.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Keep Out of the BasementWith the electricity still on, a flooded basement is not safe to walk into. The furnace, wall outlets or the cord of the pump itself could be in contact with it.
  2. Turn Off the Basement CircuitsBasement breakers should be flipped off, provided you can get to the panel without standing in water. If the panel is in the water, call the utility.
  3. Look at the Pump From a DistanceLook to see whether the pump came unplugged, its outlet tripped or the float is pinned against the pit wall. Keep your hands out of the water.
  4. Treat the Water as ContaminatedIf going down cannot be avoided, put on gloves and rubber boots, leave pets and children upstairs, and wash up after handling wet items.
  5. Move Valuables and CallTake pictures of the pump and the water level, bring valuables that are still dry upstairs, and phone us. Holding off until the basement empties is not the answer.

What Happens After You Call

Because the pumps and extraction units are ours, the broken sump does not hold up the work. Low spots, the area by the pit and the rest of the floor are cleared of water and silt.

Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and Sump Pump Failure Cleanup

This is one of the most misunderstood claims. A standard homeowners policy usually excludes water that backs up or overflows from a sump, and flood insurance is a separate policy with its own rules. Coverage often exists only if you added a water backup or sump overflow endorsement, and those carry their own limits. Your declarations page will list it, or your agent can tell you. To keep the claim file complete, our team documents the damage, the failed pump and the height of the water.

Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • A failed check valve that lets pumped water run back into the pit.
  • More water flowing into the pit than an undersized pump can move.
  • Dirt and small rocks washed into the basin that lock up the pump.
  • Losing electricity in a storm when the pump has no battery backup.
  • A float switch that is stuck, tangled or pinned against the basin wall.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Boxes and Stored Items

Cardboard boxes, furniture legs, books and fabrics soak up contaminated water quickly. Porous items left sitting in the water resist disinfecting and grow mold in a matter of days.

Finished Basement Walls

Dirty water climbs from the slab into insulation and drywall, far higher than the line you can see. The lower part that got wet is cut away, not dried, on account of the contamination.

Low Electrical Outlets

Wall outlets near the floor are sometimes submerged in a sump flood. Before those circuits are used again, an electrician needs to inspect whatever was under water.

Furnace and Water Heater

The furnace and water heater frequently sit on the same floor as the pit. Water reaching burners, controls or wiring can ruin them, and each appliance must be inspected before it is used again.
