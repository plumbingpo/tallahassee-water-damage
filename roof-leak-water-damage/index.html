Roof Leak Water Damage Help in Tallahassee

Where you see the stain and where the rain gets in are usually two different places. Rain slips under a lifted shingle, a cracked flashing joint or a tired skylight seal, then runs along the decking and rafters before it drops onto the insulation. By the time a brown ring shows on the ceiling, the attic above it has often been wet for a while. Locating all of the moisture and drying it is our job.

To be clear, roof repair is not what we do, water damage is. Inside the home, our team takes out saturated insulation, dries the wood and drywall, treats any mold and repairs the ceiling. Should the leak still be active, our crew can cover that section with a tarp for now, or time our work with the roofing contractor you pick so nothing gets sealed over an open leak.

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut Off Wet CircuitsTurn off the breaker for any light, fan or outlet that has water running through or near it, then keep your hands off it.
  2. Contain the WaterPut a pan or bucket beneath each drip, and shift rugs, electronics and furniture clear of the wet area. Plastic or towels spread on the floor will keep it from soaking.
  3. Drain a Bulging CeilingIf the ceiling is holding a pocket of water, stand to one side and poke a small hole at the lowest point so it drains into a bucket.
  4. Leave the Roof AloneClimbing on slick shingles or crawling around a dark attic is not worth the risk. Note from inside where the water is coming in and leave the rest to professionals.
  5. Photograph the DamageUse your phone to record the damaged ceiling, attic and belongings first, hold off on throwing things out until that is done, and call us to get the drying started.
Our Process

Using thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team checks wall cavities, ceilings and the attic and charts the whole route the water took, even where the room below shows nothing.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • Fasteners working loose over time, so water follows the nail shank into the attic.
  • Roof valleys where worn metal or piled debris lets water get under the covering.
  • Shingles that are cracked, curled or blown off by wind, exposing the underlayment.
  • Holes and fractures left in the roof after a branch or hailstones hit it.
  • Water backing up at the eave and under the shingles because the gutters are blocked.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Ceiling Fixtures and Wiring

Recessed cans, ceiling fans and electrical boxes are low points where roof water collects and drips through. Wet connections can short or corrode, so the circuit should stay off until it is checked.

Attic Insulation

Wet insulation mats down, stops insulating and holds water against the ceiling drywall for days. Drying is sometimes possible with fiberglass. Cellulose that is saturated generally comes out.

Drywall Ceilings

Drywall that stays wet stains, sags and loses its strength. Seams split, the tape lets go, and a heavy enough section can drop with little warning.

Inside the Walls

From the attic, a leak can drop into a stud bay and soak the wall insulation and drywall from behind. You might notice nothing more than a peeling patch or a puffy baseboard.

Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

When a leak happens suddenly and by accident, for example a branch going through the roof or wind stripping shingles, many homeowners policies pay for the water damage inside. Insurers generally turn down claims tied to wear, age or lack of upkeep, and they may handle the roof repair on different terms than the inside damage. We document the damage with photos and moisture readings for your adjuster, but coverage depends on your policy, so read it or ask your agent.

What It Costs

What Drives the Price

  • How long the roof leaked before it was found, and any mold growth.
  • How many square feet of wall and ceiling drywall have to be removed.
  • How easy the attic is to reach, how high the ceilings are, and if the roof needs a tarp before work begins.
  • Damage beyond the ceiling, such as wet floors, wall cavities or furniture.
