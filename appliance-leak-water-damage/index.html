Dealing With Appliance Leak Water Damage in Tallahassee

Three appliances cause most of the leaks we see. When the supply hose on a washing machine splits, water flows under full pressure until somebody turns it off. Dishwashers usually leak a little at a time, at the drain hose or door seal, hidden beneath the cabinets. Behind a refrigerator, the thin tube feeding the ice maker may drip for months. Each one needs a different kind of cleanup.

Our Tallahassee crew starts by pulling the appliance out and finding where the water went. In kitchens that usually means under the cabinets and beneath the flooring. Laundry room water goes into the wall at the back of the machine and the neighboring rooms, and with an upstairs laundry, the ceiling underneath. We extract what is standing, measure what has soaked in, and set drying equipment sized to the wet area.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Turn Off the ApplianceCancel the cycle, and pull the plug only when the floor and the outlet near it are dry. When either one is wet, turn the power off at the breaker.
  2. Close the Supply ValveFor a washing machine, close both the cold and hot valves on the wall at its back. Dishwashers shut off under the sink. The small valve for an ice maker line sits behind the refrigerator or underneath.
  3. Soak Up Standing WaterUse towels or a mop on whatever water is within reach, which keeps it from spreading beneath flooring and cabinets. Use a vacuum only if it is a wet and dry model.
  4. Leave the Door ShutIf the washer or dishwasher has not drained, leave it shut. Opening the door mid-cycle can release the whole load of water onto the floor.
  5. Document What FailedTake pictures of the hose, valve or fitting that failed and keep the part. Your insurer and the appliance maker may both ask to see it.

The Usual Culprits

What Causes It

  • Plastic or copper ice maker lines that kink, crack or loosen at the fitting.
  • Overloading the drum or adding excess detergent, either of which can make the machine overflow.
  • Standpipes that clog, or the drain hose from a washer coming loose from one.
  • Installation mistakes like a loose supply line connection or a dishwasher that does not sit level.
  • The water inlet valve on a washer or dishwasher failing to close, which lets the machine overfill.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Cabinet Damage

Dishwasher leaks soak cabinet sides and toe kicks from underneath. The panels swell and separate, and wet cabinet boxes can hold moisture against the wall for weeks.

Hidden Mold at the Back

The warm, dark space behind a refrigerator or dishwasher rarely gets checked. Mold on the floor and wall can be fed by a slow drip in that spot well before it is noticed.

Ceiling Below an Upstairs Laundry

In a home with an upstairs laundry, leak water passes through the floor and pools on the ceiling underneath. Drywall there sags and stains while the insulation over it holds water.

Rooms Next Door

A washer hose can release water for hours. Hallways, bedrooms and closets get wet as it travels under the walls, and carpet and pad are soaked a good distance from the laundry.

Coverage

What About Insurance?

A hose that bursts or a machine that overflows without warning is the kind of sudden and accidental event homeowners policies often cover. What is typically paid is the water damage, while the failed part and the appliance are left out. A claim is harder when an ice maker line or dishwasher seeped slowly, since coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks. How old the hose was and how it was maintained may also come up with some insurers. Before cleanup, take photos, save the failed part and read your policy.

What It Costs

What Affects the Cost

  • The number of rooms affected, counting a ceiling under a laundry on an upper floor.
  • Whether this was a sudden flood or slow seepage, and which machine it came from.
  • Presence of mold, which adds removal work in front of the drying.
  • Removal of cabinets or flooring, compared with drying them in place when that is possible.
